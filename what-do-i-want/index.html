What Do I Want

Sun, Nov 15, 2026 · Rob

A bit of a year in review of AI. I’ve been trying AI and LLMs out for lots of things - I like trying things to see what sticks, so this year I’ve been trying lots of different models and model families, agent harnesses, and churning through side projects.

Here’s a bit of a retrospective.

Worked well#

Agentic coding#

Being able to create tools I can use myself:

  • SCORM tools for Moodle
  • Bespoke concept demos (with a skill to help with formatting)

Reformatting#

Taking existing data and putting it into a format that saves time:

  • H5P content is often a series of text fields, e.g. Timeline or Crossword

Ideation#

  • Brainstorming and gap finding
  • Suggesting or refining scenario examples

Supplemental content creation#

  • Mostly images with weird analogies

Handwriting recognition#

  • Occasionally some words are just way too difficult. Grab a screen clipping of a particularly inscrutable bit of scribble and marvel.

Not so much#

  • Wholesale content creation (generally just more work)
  • Collaborative editing
  • Assessment design and rubrics

Jury still out#

Research#

Running two Hermes containers, one since early this year, one that started more recently. One is a general purpose assistant that I use to wrangle reminders, keep an eye on local events that the family might like to go to. It’s very intentional and user-driven, so lots of cron jobs, and less agent freedom. I like that I can get it to make its own tools, it has a memory store, and I can drop a couple of links or ideas into Discord and it can go find some avenues to explore.

The second container is more recent. I wanted to see what it could do with relatively little guidance beyond some initial investigation questions, a local static site generator, and a couple of cron-driven wake-ups a day with different instructions, like reading papers, writing up memos and reports, and writing simple prototypes.

Honestly the research minion has been a bit of a disappointment. It’s dug up a few interesting papers, but the memos and prototypes have been lacklustre. Often I find I’m having to prompt it more than I wanted to, and I’ve gone through more than one round of modifications to make its language less frustratingly LLM-like.

Small local models#

I dug out my old gaming PC, stuck a couple of GTX 1060s in it that I had lying around, and have been experimenting with what I can do with local models. I have 9GB of VRAM between them, so I can run some 4 bit quants of up to 12B models pretty slowly, and 4-9B models at reasonable speeds.

One of the Hermes containers uses it for vectorisation, and I have a couple of vision models on it for extraction and OCR. The thing I’ve enjoyed using it for the most has been Wordcraft - a local web app for my kids to get some regular writing practice in, with various prompts and helper tools set up to give them feedback and advice. The kids have generally been pretty receptive, and the quality of the feedback as been… passable. I had to plumb in LanguageTool to do spelling and grammar though because, well, small models are woeful at that, and hallucinate details all over the shop. Even when told about errors, it took a fair bit of harness code to set things up in a way that didn’t let them take a legitimate error and state that it was in a different part of the writing.

That project has a blog post in it, but I want to nail down a few more ideas first.

Proof reading and refining#

  • Feedback feels like it shifts my arguments away from my intended point, but at the same time feels perfectly reasonable.
  • At one point I asked it to analyse my writing history, and (apart from blowing a bunch of smoke up my butt) pulled a bunch of meaning out of my posts that was definitely not there to begin with.
Info

Now that I think about that last point, it feels a lot like when I was in English class in high school, talking about connections and meanings that I was fairly certain the original author had not intended but was no longer around to put up a defense.
